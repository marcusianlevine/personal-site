## Stages

### Two roads to the same first date.

The apps don't handle the first stages of dating. They trap you in them. Swipe, match, text, schedule, wait, and only on the date do you find out whether you feel good around her. In person, that answer arrives in the first two minutes, and every step trains a skill you keep.

Caption: The time labels are typical, not measured. The freeze at "say hello" is where everyone starts, and it's where the exercises below start too.

## Before you say a word

Creepiness has a formula: hidden agenda plus ignored signals. Wanting to talk to her isn't creepy. Pretending you don't is where it gets weird. So be honest about why you're there, and read the room before you open your mouth.

> Is she open to connection? How does she respond to your approach?

| What you see | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Headphones in, turned away, buried in her phone | Busy. Not a no to you, a no to everyone. | Leave her be. |
| Mid-conversation, in a hurry, at work | Not now. If she's working, she can't walk away from you. | Chat if it's natural. Don't pursue. |
| Short answers, no eye contact after your hello | She's said no. | "No worries, enjoy your day." Mean it. Go. |
| Eyes up, open to the room, lingering, looks back twice | Green light. | Say hello. |
| You honestly can't tell | Unclear is not a no. | One short hello. Her response is the answer. |

## The first sixty seconds

- **Say hello, with the truth.** "Hi, I noticed you and wanted to say hi. I'm \[your name\]." That's the whole opener. The honesty is what makes it land.
- **Ask one real question.** About right now: the book, the dog, the thing she's carrying, what she's up to today. Curiosity, not a line.
- **Listen.** Reply to what she actually said, not to what you planned to say next.
	- Notice your level of attraction: how excited are you to continue the conversation with her?
	- Notice her body language: does she turn toward you, hold eye contact, ask you something back?
- **Say what you want. **If it's going well: "I'd like to continue this conversation another time. Can I get your number?" Simple, direct, and easy to turn down.
- If she turns you down:
	- **Accept the "no" gracefully.** "No worries, have a nice day." Then actually leave. Lingering reads as creepy.
- If she seems excited:
	- **Make a clean exit. "**Great, I'll text you." Then go. A clear next step followed by a quick exit beats lingering.

## The ladder: one step at a time

If you don't feel comfortable jumping straight to the full approach outlined above, start small. Stay on a rung until your body is bored there, then climb. Bored means trained.

- **Rung 1.** Ten seconds with a stranger, no goal. Ask for a recommendation, comment on the queue, hold the door and be polite.
- **Rung 2.** A genuine compliment to a stranger, about something she chose. The jacket, the book, the bike. Not her body. Leave without asking for anything.
- **Rung 3.** One real conversation with a woman you're *not* attracted to. A colleague, a neighbor, the person who makes your coffee.
- **Rung 4.** Run the full sixty second approach outlined above.

## The freeze, and what to do with it

The freeze before you say hi is not a character flaw. It means you care - you're not a creep.

But it's also your untrained nervous system getting in the way of connection. Dating apps don't prepare you for in-person connection. The freeze lives in your body, not your head, so that's where you work with it.

**Before an approach:** feet on the floor. Three slow breaths, exhale longer than the inhale. Remind your body that it's safe right now. Then go, before your head catches up.

**After a no:** nothing to fix. Log it below. A no is information - not a personal judgement about you.

**After a yes:** Nice. Log it. Notice what your body did.
